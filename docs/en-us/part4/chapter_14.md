# Chapter 14 (Epilogue): Standing at the Crossroads of 2026 — Kill the APP, or Let Code Grow a Soul?

March 2026. Silicon Valley.

OpenAI released GPT-5.

Sam Altman stood on the stage at the launch event, wearing a gray hoodie. There was both excitement and a hint of caution in his eyes. He knew that the release of this product would change everything.

GPT-5 is no longer just a chatbot but an "agent." It can browse the web, operate software, and execute tasks. You tell it "help me book a flight to New York," and it opens the airline website, queries flights, chooses the most suitable one, and completes the booking.

You don't need to open any APP. You just need to talk to GPT-5.

The APP is obsolete.

---

**Meanwhile, Beijing.**

Liang Wenfeng stood in DeepSeek's office, watching the data jump on the screen. DeepSeek-V4's downloads were soaring. He wore a simple T-shirt, his eyes focused and calm.

DeepSeek-V4 can not only understand your intent and call various services but also run locally. You tell it "help me order takeout," and it opens Meituan, chooses your favorite restaurant, places the order, and pays.

You don't need to open the Meituan APP. You just need to talk to DeepSeek.

This is the ultimate form of the "intent-driven network": AI becomes the entry point for all services, and APPs become the backend of AI.

Are APPs being "killed"?

---

Standing at the crossroads of 2026, we see two paths.

**Path One: The Death of the APP**

On this path, AI becomes the only entry point.

Users no longer open APPs, no longer browse the web, no longer click buttons. Users only need to state their intent, and AI completes everything.

This means:

The death of search: Users no longer search; AI directly gives answers. Google, Baidu, and Bing—the traffic of these search engines will drop significantly.

The death of e-commerce: Users no longer browse product lists; AI directly recommends the most suitable products. Taobao, Amazon, and JD.com—the traffic of these e-commerce platforms will drop significantly.

The death of content platforms: Users no longer scroll through short videos or read articles; AI directly generates personalized content. Douyin, TikTok, and YouTube—the traffic of these content platforms will drop significantly.

The death of the APP: Users no longer download APPs or learn how to use software. All functions are completed through AI conversation.

Intermediaries are zeroed out.

This is a "murder of intermediaries." AI replaces all intermediary platforms, becoming the only interface between users and services.

---

**Path Two: The Rebirth of the APP**

On this path, AI becomes a plugin for APPs.

APPs still exist, but they integrate AI capabilities. Users open APPs, and AI helps users complete tasks.

This means:

The evolution of search: Google and Baidu add AI-generated answers to search results. Search changes from "finding links" to "finding answers."

The evolution of e-commerce: Taobao and Amazon launch AI shopping assistants. Users describe their needs, and AI recommends products.

The evolution of content platforms: Douyin and TikTok launch AI content generation tools. Creators use AI to assist creation, and platforms use AI to recommend content.

The evolution of the APP: The APP is no longer static software but a dynamic service. AI makes APPs smarter and more personalized.

This is an "evolution of platforms." AI is not replacing platforms but enhancing them.

---

No matter which path is taken, the world is moving toward a dual-core structure.

Sam Altman believes in AGI. He believes that AGI will solve all the problems facing humanity: disease, poverty, climate change.

Altman's ambition goes beyond this. He believes that AGI will lead humanity into a new era. He invests in nuclear fusion, longevity research, and space exploration. He believes that AGI is the key to the future.

But Altman also faces questions. OpenAI's transformation from a non-profit organization to a for-profit company has sparked criticism of "betraying its original intention." GPT-4's safety issues, copyright issues, and employment impacts have also raised widespread concerns.

Altman's response is: "We need to be cautious, but we also need to move forward. The benefits of AGI outweigh the risks."

By 2026, OpenAI is training GPT-5. According to internal sources, GPT-5 is close to the threshold of AGI. It can reach or exceed human levels on most intellectual tasks.

If GPT-5 truly achieves AGI, it will be the most important moment in human history.

---

Liang Wenfeng believes that computing efficiency is the key to AI development. Under the background of computing power blockade, Chinese companies cannot obtain the most advanced GPUs and can only make up for it through efficiency.

"Americans have their advantages, and we also have our advantages," Liang Wenfeng said. "We have the world's largest manufacturing industry, the most complete supply chain, and the richest application scenarios. These advantages can be transformed into AI advantages."

DeepSeek-V4 was released in March 2026, once again shocking the global AI community. Its performance surpasses GPT-4o, but the training cost is only one percent of GPT-4o.

More importantly, DeepSeek-V4 chose to be completely open source. Anyone can download, use, and modify this model. This is a challenge to closed-source models and a push for AI democratization.

Liang Wenfeng's vision is: to make AI inclusive, so that everyone can enjoy the benefits of AI. "AI should not be the patent of a few big companies," he said. "It should be the wealth of all humanity."

---

Standing at the crossroads of 2026, I think of the first line of code I wrote in 2009.

At that time, I didn't know what AI was, what a large language model was, or what embodied intelligence was. I only knew that code could change the world.

Now, code is really changing the world.

But code is not omnipotent. Code needs hardware to run, data to train, and people to write.

The destiny of code depends on the people holding the keyboard.

We are the people holding the keyboard.

---

**Five years later, 2031**: AGI is achieved. AI can complete most intellectual tasks, and humans focus on creative and emotional work.

**Ten years later, 2036**: Robots are widespread. They do housework, take care of the elderly, and work in factories. Humans are freed from physical labor.

**Twenty years later, 2046**: Brain-computer interfaces mature. Humans can directly communicate with AI, and the boundary between humans and machines begins to blur.

**Thirty years later, 2056**: When AI has its own consciousness, when robots have their own emotions, what is the definition of "human"?

The destiny of code depends on the people holding the keyboard.

And the story never ends.
